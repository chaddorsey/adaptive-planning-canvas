The Concord Consortium

The Adaptive Planning Canvas

Fiscal Year 2027 · Draft of August 7, 2026
Creed
Good STEM learning is practice for leading a good life
Vision

We envision a world where all students and teachers use effective digital resources to engage deeply, justly, and equitably with STEM concepts and practices in varied personal, cultural, and social contexts.

Three-Year Mission
Commercial Position

A short-list provider of top simulations and tools for high-quality STEM learning experiences

Customer Purpose

The friendly, amazing guide for exploring STEM topics and creating “a-ha” learning moments

Cultural Perception

Technology used well is a critical component of effective STEM learning

  • Number of B2B clients

  • Number of publications
  • Number of “project translations” — traceable outputs from project work into client implementations
  • Number of repeats / referrals (to confirm)
  • Average size of business engagement
  • Number of significant non-federal funders

Strategic Themes
How the three-year mission is realized across four strategic fields of play — each with its own working hypotheses, ongoing upkeep, backlog of bets, and one-year intentions.
Theme #1

Successful B2B Service Engagements

Iterative Hypotheses
Key indicators, conditions, models, causation, strategic trade-offs
  • AssumptionProviders desire to make interactive content and are willing to pay for it
  • AssumptionProviders value interactive content that is of high pedagogical quality, and can discriminate among levels of quality
  • IndicatorConversations with potential clients regularly lead to follow-up calls that include demos and concrete suggestions for creation and/or integration
  • IndicatorConversations lead to concrete, paid “first pancake” projects with outcomes that satisfy both parties
  • ExpectationOur simulations are likely to be the point of obvious contact and engagement for most providers, and integrations are likely to be light touch
  • ExpectationA small number of providers will understand and be interested in our tools or broader integration on initial conversation. However, as relationships with other providers progress, a meaningful fraction will begin to understand the value of these additional options
  • ModelMost of our contracts are likely to involve a mix of modification of existing simulations (modified 10–20% from the existing base) and creation of new simulations (created with ~75% new code based on existing engines / infrastructure)
  • AssumptionWe will need to monitor the landscape for implications of blowback against screen-time exposure and create advocacy options to emphasize the critical importance of “quality screen time” for STEM learning
  • Add hypothesis
Upkeep Efforts
“Keep the lights on” work
  • Regular influx of meetings with new prospects
  • Regular update connections with existing clients
  • Steady, reliable management of development for client engagements
  • Regular monitoring of pipeline against goals and check-ins with Matt
  • Adjusting messaging and outreach; iteratively developing and improving marketing materials and approaches
  • Add effort
Bet Backlog
New and/or uncertain activities
  • Development of AI-based innovations, frameworks, and approaches that augment our existing work
  • New partnerships with AI providers / startups (e.g. Forever Learning, Fermi)
  • Development of coherent, modular frameworks and architectures to support a full variety of STEM learning experiences
  • Development of integration infrastructure (LTI, cross-talk, embedding capabilities)
  • Improvement / development of logging and assessment capabilities
  • Add bet
One-Year Intentions
Including key metrics · by Oct. 1, 2027
  • Bring in and consume $1M in new B2B revenue
  • Land six first-pancake engagements (~$25–$50K) with B2B clients
  • Land and complete three full B2B engagements (~$200–$500K) that leave clients satisfied
  • Establish one or more synergistic AI-related partnerships
  • Develop three key showcase-able AI capabilities or features viable for use with B2B clients
  • Add intention

Theme #2

Sustaining Key Products

Iterative Hypotheses
Key indicators, conditions, models, causation, strategic trade-offs
  • AssumptionOur independent / stand-alone products or platforms hold revenue-generating capability
  • AssumptionRevenue generation around these products will involve more diverse business models than those for our standard B2B work
  • IndicatorWe are able to frame independent value propositions for these products as discrete entities that resonate with others external to the organization
  • IndicatorWe can identify both distinguishable user bases and revenue-generating bases for these products
  • Trade-offPlausible paths for revenue generation may involve activities of a different nature than our B2B model and work (e.g., direct sales or district-based contracts); we will need to consider the ramifications of following these through, including the potential need for new or different partnerships
  • ModelRevenue involving these products may have overlap with our B2B work; these needs may generate pressures that conflict with product-based development
  • Trade-offWork to define and develop these products and their value propositions is distinct from work on cultivating and evolving our B2B model, and the needed capacity is different as well; work in this direction holds an opportunity-cost trade-off
  • Add hypothesis
Upkeep Efforts
“Keep the lights on” work
  • User discovery / value proposition work
  • Maintenance of existing user communities and showcase resources
  • Ongoing progress along product development pipeline
  • Add effort
Bet Backlog
New and/or uncertain activities
  • Developing showcase resources / exemplars
  • Creation of key examples or frameworks to support integration, potentially on spec
  • Experiments with district needs assessments and relationships
  • Add bet

  • Outputs from full user discovery sessions for each
  • Proposed models and hypothetical (or better) value propositions for each

Theme #3

Deploying NSF / Federal Funding as an Applied Innovation Engine

Iterative Hypotheses
Key indicators, conditions, models, causation, strategic trade-offs
  • AssumptionNSF funding will continue to be a viable source of revenue for innovative proposals
  • AssumptionWe can single out strategic aspects of the infrastructure we deem essential to furthering our B2B and Product work and can build and test them as part of the work of awarded NSF projects
  • AssumptionThe research conducted in NSF projects can be leveraged as an asset for reliability, effectiveness, and credibility of our infrastructure / products and the expertise provided in our B2B services
  • IndicatorWe can visibly trace selected elements of our grander vision and infrastructure into ongoing NSF-sponsored projects
  • IndicatorWe can visibly trace trajectories in which products and infrastructure elements cultivated in NSF projects become incorporated into B2B and Product work
  • Trade-offNSF work and research often involves inherently lengthy timelines that may slow the development of budding / promising innovations
  • Trade-offNSF work represents an opportunity cost against B2B and product-facing revenue activity; work across the organization needs to balance these arms consciously and strategically
  • Add hypothesis
Upkeep Efforts
“Keep the lights on” work
  • Regular, ongoing NSF / federal proposal development and submission
  • Conducting the regular work of projects satisfactorily, including project cycles and reporting
  • Remaining engaged with colleague collaborators as part of project work
  • Producing, publishing, and presenting research and regularly networking with colleagues, the larger research community, and NSF and federal funding representatives
  • Add effort
Bet Backlog
New and/or uncertain activities
  • Participation in new and/or unfamiliar federal funding programs (e.g., different NSF directorates, different agencies — Dept. of Labor, IES, DoW)
  • Employing explicit, strategic planning and internal gating of proposal development to ensure alignment with greater organizational needs
  • New forms of research (e.g., large-scale RCTs, evaluation) that reinforce our B2B and Product activities
  • Add bet
One-Year Intentions
Including key metrics
  • Create an organization-wide “big board” of infrastructure and capacity needs, visible to and understood by all members of the organization, by Oct. 1, 2026
  • Enact a process for proposal development that explicitly aligns individual proposals to one or more identified needs from the “big board” by Nov. 1, 2026
  • Ensure that each PI / writer submits two or more NSF / federal proposals in FY27, with at least one of the two submitted by Dec. 31, 2026
  • Add intention

Theme #4

Securing Private Revenue as a Flexibility Enabler

Iterative Hypotheses
Key indicators, conditions, models, causation, strategic trade-offs
  • AssumptionWe are able to cast our work across the organization successfully into themes that transcend individual projects and are understandable by and attractive to external private funders
  • AssumptionWe can enact and support high-quality, ongoing networking and referral activity at a level sufficient to open doors with a large number of funders across types and sectors
  • IndicatorAt least 30% of our private funding conversations involve representatives from industry and private companies
  • IndicatorMultiple private funders invite us to submit proposals of six figures or greater
  • ConditionsWe cast our work successfully in ways that match current funders' interests and priorities, including new directions such as pathways and workforce development
  • Trade-offWork developing relationships can be a slow-payoff investment and involves short-term opportunity cost
  • Trade-offIdentifying and remaining attractive to funders can pose the potential of mission creep; on the other hand, investigating new activities and directions may at times be necessary or even desirable — regular reflection and re-inspection is essential to remain mission aligned and keep these trade-offs in check
  • Add hypothesis
Upkeep Efforts
“Keep the lights on” work
  • Regular, repeated conversations and correspondence with funder representatives
  • Ongoing development and refinement of case statements for overall organization and organizational themes of work
  • Upkeep of reporting and connection, including forwarding of successes and “marketing” information to continually maintain reputation and funder awareness
  • Continual solicitation of connections across all sources, from Board to staff to collaborators and partners
  • Regular development and refining of case statements, briefings, and proposal materials to maintain funder interaction and evolve relationships
  • Cognizance and regular evaluation of released solicitations, competitions, and proposal opportunities from private funders
  • Add effort
Bet Backlog
New and/or uncertain activities
  • Submission of bids and vendor paperwork for listing on rosters of state agencies
  • Cultivating relationships with state and federal lawmakers / representatives
  • Maintaining awareness of potential for individual / major giving as a funding source
  • Add bet
One-Year Intentions
Including key metrics
  • Secure $750K–$1M in new private funding by Sept. 30, 2027
  • Receive invitations for proposal submission from 12–15 different private funders
  • Hold conversations with over 25 distinct potential private funders across foundations and industry by Sept. 30, 2027
  • Add intention
